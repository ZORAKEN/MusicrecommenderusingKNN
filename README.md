# MusicrecommenderusingKNN
Evaluation Metrics for a KNN-Based Recommender System

A K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN) recommender system predicts a user's preferences by identifying users or items with similar characteristics and recommending items based on these nearest neighbours. The quality of the generated recommendations is commonly evaluated using the following ranking metrics:

Recall@K measures the proportion of relevant items that are successfully retrieved within the top K recommendations generated by the KNN model. It evaluates how effectively the KNN algorithm identifies items that the user is actually interested in. A higher Recall@K indicates better coverage of relevant recommendations.
Precision@K measures the proportion of the top K recommendations that are actually relevant to the user. For a KNN recommender, it reflects how accurately the nearest-neighbour approach selects useful items while minimising irrelevant recommendations. High Precision@K indicates that most recommended items match user preferences.
F1@K is the harmonic mean of Precision@K and Recall@K, providing a balanced evaluation of the KNN recommender. It is particularly useful when both retrieving a large number of relevant items and maintaining recommendation accuracy are equally important.
Mean Average Precision (MAP) evaluates the overall ranking performance of the KNN recommender by considering both the relevance of recommended items and their positions in the ranked recommendation list. It rewards KNN models that place relevant items closer to the top, where users are more likely to interact with them.
Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) measures the reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant item recommended by the KNN algorithm. It assesses how quickly the recommender presents a useful recommendation, with higher MRR values indicating that relevant items appear earlier in the recommendation list.
Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG) evaluates the ranking quality of the KNN recommender by considering both the relevance level of recommended items and their positions. Relevant items appearing near the top receive greater weight than those ranked lower. The score is normalised to allow fair comparison across different users and recommendation lists, making it particularly suitable for evaluating ranked outputs from KNN-based recommendation systems.
